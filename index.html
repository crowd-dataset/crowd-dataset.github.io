CROWDCity Road Observations With Dashcams

35,570 hours of ordinary driving through 10,237 cities.

CROWD collects front-facing dashcam footage from YouTube in 238 countries and territories, cut by hand into continuous drives and labelled, with 142 million road users tracked, for research on how people and vehicles share the street.

About

Ordinary streets, filmed from the driver's seat, in every kind of city.

  • Hand-curated. Every segment is one continuous, unedited stretch of front-facing driving, checked by a person.
  • Labelled. Time of day and the vehicle the camera rides on, for every segment.
  • Tracked. YOLO11x detections with BoT-SORT tracks for all 80 MS-COCO classes.
  • Shared responsibly. The dataset holds video identifiers and time stamps only; the videos themselves are not part of it.

Curation

How a segment gets in

Every segment is one continuous, unedited stretch of ordinary driving. People choose the segments and check them by hand.

Pseudo-labels

YOLO11x tracking on every segment

Each segment comes with a CSV of automatic detections for all 80 MS-COCO classes, with track IDs, so persons, cars, bicycles, motorcycles, buses and trucks can be counted per minute and followed over time.

What is left out

Checked again by hand

52,838candidate cuts reviewed by a person
≈ 59%of candidates were not real cuts, so nothing was cut automatically
  1. Find candidates. FFmpeg scene-change detection flags moments in each segment where the picture changes abruptly.
  2. Review. A reviewer watches two seconds either side of each candidate and marks it as a real cut, not a cut, or unavailable.
  3. Look for more. The reviewer also notes footage that is not at normal speed and removes advertisements cut into the drive.
  4. Apply. Confirmed cuts split their segment, the detection files are split at the same frames, and pieces that become too short are dropped.
