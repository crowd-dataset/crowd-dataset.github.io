How the world crosses the road

Pedestrians crossing in front of dashcams in a hundred cities on six continents: how fast they walk across, in metres per second, and how long they stand still before stepping onto the road, set beside each city's traffic, wealth and road safety.

                Method

                From a box on a screen to metres per second

                A dashcam sees pixels, not metres. The speed model is calibrated on the Waymo Open Dataset, where lidar measures how fast each pedestrian really walks, and then frozen.

                1. Track. YOLO11x and BoT-SORT find and follow people in every segment of a city's footage.
                2. Find crossings. A track counts as a road crossing when it moves across the middle of the image in front of the camera, with its feet on the road.
                3. Measure speed. A model trained on Waymo turns the box's motion into metres per second.
                4. Measure the wait. Position checks find how long the person stood still, moving less than a tenth of their height, before stepping onto the road.
                5. Read the road. Optionally, SegFormer reads the surface under the feet, so speed and waiting are measured from the moment the person is on the carriageway.
                6. Relate. City means are set beside population, wealth, literacy, inequality, traffic and road deaths.

                Checked on tracks it never saw

                The frozen speed model on 27 untouched Waymo validation tracks: mean error 0.13 m/s, root mean square error 0.21 m/s, bias −0.05 m/s.
